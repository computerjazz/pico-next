## Range anxiety
One of the greatest luxuries is to have your needs met without having to think. To exist without the need to plan ahead, and without dreading what might happen if you don't. This of course applies mostly to the "big stuff", but it exists in life's minutia too. 

I want a phone that's smooth and snappy, with a bright screen, and I want the battery to last forever. But mere minutes after I wake up and unplug, the battery drops to 99%, and the clock starts ticking. The glass is no longer completely full. The range anxiety creeps in.

Of course, the healthy response is to accept this reality and plan around it. Nothing lasts forever so enjoy it while you have it,  etc. I clearly struggle with this. But solving the charging issue, or at least optimizing it, has proven to be an interesting design problem.

I have a friend who got really into LoRA + Bluetoolh Low Energy (BLE), creating networks of little devices spread over large areas that spent most of their time asleep. They'd wake up, sense their environment, chirp a quick message to be picked up miles away, and go back to sleep. The little batteries lasted a long time, _years_ even!

Amazing! And yet...

What happens after that year or two? Sure I could set a calendar reminder and try not to think about it, but that's not how my mind works. I demand _full autopilot_!

Which is why it feels so good to when the power demands and the usage patterns of a gadget mesh naturally.

Big appliances that don't move have an easy answer—plug 'em in and forget about 'em. We've come to accept that phones last a day and the charger on the nightstand works out fine. Laptops go to coffee shops for an hour or two and come home to spend the rest of the day plugged in (you people screen sharing at home with a thin red sliver of battery are maniacs!).
